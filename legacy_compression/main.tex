\title{Choosing chart detail by preserving two-sample differences}
\author{Mohamed Elgendi\\Khalifa University, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates}
\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
A chart can be simple because little detail is needed, or because important detail has been discarded. We introduce a resolution-dependent procedure that makes this distinction explicit for two numerical samples. The procedure compares the empirical quantile contrast with reconstructions from means, five-number summaries, bins, densities and individual observations. It identifies the smallest representation that preserves this comparison within a declared tolerance. A positive selection score reports the percentage reduction in retained scalar values, credited only to qualifying representations; a catalogue of 15 familiar chart families is screened for task applicability, and eligible families are reported in descending score order. Raw observations define the zero-reduction reference. Equivalent visual encodings remain explicit. A revised audit of 127 comparisons from nine medical and digital-health resources classified 61 as adequately represented by means, 34 by five-number summaries and 32 as requiring finer detail; 40 classifications had conditional bootstrap agreement below 0.80. Increasing the bootstrap count from 99 to 999 changed four selected configurations, whereas two alternative payload-count conventions changed 15 and 18. Across 1,080 simulated training--test pairs spanning nine conditions, the adaptive selection passed a frozen training tolerance in \FrozenPass\% of test samples. Known-population evaluation exposed a separate sampling limitation that numerical reconstruction alone cannot remove. The contribution is an auditable method for choosing representation detail, with explicit resolution, encoding and provenance limits. It does not establish improved human reading accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
\textbf{Preserve the comparison before choosing the chart.} A researcher comparing two samples must decide whether averages are enough, whether quartiles add necessary information, or whether a display should retain finer distributional detail. This decision is often made by habit or by choosing a familiar chart family. Yet a bar chart can hide structure, while an elaborate distribution display can retain detail irrelevant to the question.\cite{weissgerber2015} Scientific visualization training needs an explanation of what a summary preserves and what it discards.\cite{elgendi2017,elgendi2020}

Automated visualization is an established field. Mackinlay formalized graphical design; task taxonomies describe analytic intent; Draco expresses design knowledge as constraints; and VizML learns visualization choices from a large collection of dataset--visualization pairs.\cite{mackinlay1986,amar2005,draco,vizml} Quantile contrasts are also established tools for understanding how groups differ beyond their means.\cite{rousselet2017} The contribution here is neither a new quantile statistic nor a universal ranking of chart families. It is an explicit connection between a two-sample contrast, a tolerance-constrained ranking by retained detail, and familiar displays of the qualifying representations.

The proposed procedure separates three decisions. First, define the comparison that must be preserved. Second, determine the numerical detail required at a declared resolution. Third, select a visual encoding of that representation. The present algorithm addresses the second decision. Histograms and binned heatmaps can retain the same numbers, and bars and mean dots can encode the same two means. Their numerical equivalence does not imply perceptual equivalence.\cite{cleveland1984}

Our default target is the central difference between marginal quantile functions. This target is useful when the descriptive question is how the lower, middle and upper parts of two samples differ. It is unsuitable when the primary question concerns rare tail events, individual paired changes, associations, temporal patterns or uncertainty intervals. The data determine the approximation error; the scientific question determines whether that error is relevant. The method therefore returns its objective, tolerance, qualifying representations and unresolved encoding choices together.

\section*{Results}
\subsection*{A numerical contract makes simplification inspectable}
Let $x$ and $y$ be two finite numerical samples on the same measurement scale. Over a declared probability interval $\mathcal P$, the target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
\widehat\Delta(p)=\widehat F_y^{-1}(p)-\widehat F_x^{-1}(p),\qquad p\in\mathcal P.
\end{equation}
The default interval is $[0.05,0.95]$. At each rank, the contrast compares the corresponding values of the two groups. A constant contrast is compatible with a location shift; variation across ranks requires information beyond a single difference. This is a descriptive empirical target, not a causal treatment effect or a quantile function of within-person differences.

Each candidate representation $c$ has a declared decoder, retained scalar count $N_c$, and reconstructed contrast $\widetilde\Delta_c$. Its error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
E_c=\sup_{p\in\mathcal P}|\widetilde\Delta_c(p)-\widehat\Delta(p)|.
\end{equation}
A representation qualifies when $E_c\leq\tau$, where $\tau$ is the declared tolerance in measurement units. The reported frontier contains candidates for which no other candidate has both no greater error and no greater retained size, with at least one strict improvement. For a specified tolerance, the selected set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
\mathcal C^*(\tau)=\underset{c:E_c\leq\tau}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\;(N_c,E_c),
\end{equation}
where the pair is ordered lexicographically: first minimize retained values, then error. Exact numerical ties are retained. The selection score in equation~\ref{eq:score} reports this same compactness objective positively. It does not replace the preservation constraint with a weighted average.

The numerical catalogue contains means, five-number summaries, common-bin probabilities, discretized densities and observations (Table~\ref{tab:contracts}). Histogram and density configurations overlap in retained size at 18, 34, 66 and 130 scalars. Five-number summaries retain ten values; group means are not added to their cost because the decoder does not use them. These changes prevent the original catalogue's minimum density size and optional mean markers from determining the ranking in advance. The contracts remain choices and are tested as such.

\subsection*{Screen chart families before comparing their scores}
The declared catalogue contains bar, line, scatter, pie, histogram, box, heatmap, stacked bar, area, bubble, dot, treemap, choropleth, violin and error-bar/interval plots. These are 15 familiar families, not a measured ranking of worldwide usage. Chart eligibility depends on both the input structure and the scientific question. Supplementary Table S3 states every family's purpose, eligibility and contract; Supplementary Figure S7 illustrates all seven implemented encodings on the same observations.

For the present marginal two-sample quantile contrast, seven families have implemented contracts: mean bars; histograms; five-number box plots; binned probability heatmaps; mean or raw dots; sampled-density violins; and five-number interval plots. The interval display uses a median point, a thick interquartile interval and a thin minimum--maximum interval. It represents observed variability, not a confidence interval or standard error. The heatmap contains one row per group and one column per common bin, with group probabilities encoded by colour. These families comprise 21 encoding settings that share the 14 numerical configurations; equivalent encodings are scored once numerically and retain identical scores and discrepancies.

The remaining eight families are listed as not applicable to this declared task, rather than assigned zero scores. Line and area charts require an ordered-trend objective; scatter and bubble charts require a joint-association objective; pie and stacked bar charts require a declared composition objective; treemaps require hierarchy and composition; choropleths require geographic identifiers and a geographic objective. Some can be repurposed after transforming the data, but those transformations and alternative targets are not evaluated here. Paired sample indices alone do not turn a marginal contrast into an association analysis. Thus the implementation screens all 15 families and optimizes the eligible subset; it does not claim a universal optimizer for all 15 tasks.

Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} shows the workflow using two columns of WDBC radius-standard-error measurements.\cite{wdbc} All 15 chart families are screened for compatibility with the same marginal-comparison task; their eligible settings share 14 numerical configurations. A candidate first has to preserve the specified quantile contrast within tolerance; its selection score then credits the reduction in retained scalar values relative to the raw observations. All numerically optimal chart families remain in the results. One is displayed using the declared catalogue order to resolve exact numerical ties; this is a deterministic presentation rule, not evidence of superior visual effectiveness. In this example, eight bins retain 18 of 569 scalar values and score 96.8. The histogram and binned heatmap share that optimum; the histogram is illustrated. Raw observations retain all 569 values and score zero, although their empirical reconstruction is exact.

The compactness objective in equation~\ref{eq:selection} asks how much numerical detail is sufficient at a declared tolerance. The error--size frontier and its resolution path appear in Supplementary Figure~S1. Decreasing tolerance cannot reduce the minimum qualifying size for a fixed catalogue. Increasing tolerance can permit a simpler representation, but cannot establish that it is easier to read. The figures, audit and simulations use the same selection rule.



\subsection*{Resolution must be declared, not hidden inside a score}
Two resolution policies answer different questions. A fixed budget $\tau=\eta s$ asks how much detail is needed at a specified fraction of the pooled within-group scale $s$. A sampling-adaptive budget asks which empirical differences exceed an estimated sampling allowance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
\tau_{\mathrm{adaptive}}=\max\left\{0.1s,\operatorname{quantile}_{0.95,b}\left[\sup_{p\in\mathcal P}|\widehat\Delta_b(p)-\widehat\Delta(p)|\right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
The default audit uses 999 ordinary bootstrap resamples. The bootstrap percentile is a descriptive resolution choice; it is not asserted to be a calibrated confidence band. In small or noisy samples it may permit coarse summaries. Accordingly, an adaptive pass means that approximation error is below this allowance, not that distributions are similar or the population difference is known precisely.

This distinction has a simple mathematical basis. If $\Delta$ denotes the population contrast and $U=\|\widehat\Delta-\Delta\|_\infty$, then the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
\|\widetilde\Delta_c-\Delta\|_\infty\leq E_c+U.
\end{equation}
Reducing representation error does not remove sampling error. If a separately justified simultaneous sampling bound were available, it could be combined with $E_c$; the empirical bootstrap allowance used here does not itself establish such a bound. Supplementary Methods give the selection properties, scope counterexamples and numerical checks.



\subsection*{A primary audit across nine resources separates detail from display}
The primary audit contains 127 feature comparisons from nine medical and digital-health resources: WDBC, Cleveland heart disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, liver disease, retinopathy, smartphone activity, BIDMC monitor numerics and wrist-exercise ECG references.\cite{wdbc,heart,failure,kidney,liver,retina,har,bidmc,wrist} The 22 Parkinsons comparisons are reported separately because 32 parsed identifier groups cannot be reconciled with the documented 31 participants.\cite{parkinsons} No groups were merged by assumption.

Under the compactness analysis at the default adaptive resolution, means were sufficient in 61 comparisons, five-number summaries were needed in 34, and finer detail was needed in 32 (Supplementary Fig.~S2a). These explanatory strata ask whether the specified mean and five-number reconstructions pass. They do not force the winning representation: a binned candidate can use the same number of values as a five-number summary while producing a smaller error. The selected representations were means in 61 cases, five-number summaries in 29, bins in 33 and discretized densities in four. All primary and excluded results are listed separately in Supplementary Table S2.

Forty primary comparisons (31.5\%) had conditional bootstrap stratum agreement below 0.80; median agreement was 0.900. This diagnostic holds the original tolerance fixed and reuses the resamples that estimated it. It is not an independent probability that a winning chart is correct. A second, independent 999-resample stream changed one of 127 selected configurations. Increasing nested bootstrap draws from 99 to 999 changed four configurations; increasing from 499 to 999 changed one. The median absolute relative tolerance differences were 2.32\%, 0.45\% and 1.15\% for 99 draws, 499 draws and the independent stream, respectively (Supplementary Fig.~S2b). These findings support numerical convergence on this audit while preserving the distinction between Monte Carlo variability and data-sampling instability.

The resolution policy had a larger effect. Relative to the default, allowance percentiles of 0.80, 0.90 and 0.99 changed 25, 16 and 17 selected configurations. Changing the central interval to $[0.01,0.99]$ or $[0.10,0.90]$ changed 38 or 32. A fixed $0.1s$ budget changed all 127 selections and required distribution detail in every case. These are substantive dependencies, not nuisances to be averaged away. The resolution path makes them inspectable (Supplementary Fig.~S2c,d).



\subsection*{A positive score for qualifying simplification}
Figure~\ref{fig:success} lists the seven eligible chart families for each of three public-database comparisons, reporting the best setting of each. All 15 screened families and the eight exclusions are documented in Supplementary Table S3. The starting mean display is shown beside the selected representation, using identical observations and group colours. Let $N_{\mathrm{raw}}=n_x+n_y$. For finite reconstruction discrepancy, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
S_c=\begin{cases}
100\max\{0,1-N_c/N_{\mathrm{raw}}\}, & E_c\leq\tau,\\
0, & E_c>\tau.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus, a score of 90 means that the representation removes 90\% of the counted scalar payload while meeting the declared preservation tolerance. It does not mean 90\% reading accuracy, 90\% information preservation or a 90\% probability of correctness. Raw observations score zero because no values are removed. A finite nonqualifying candidate also scores zero, but carries an explicit rejection marker; a failed numerical computation is unavailable, not assigned a measured score. A score of 100 would require zero retained values and is unattainable for the nonempty representations in this catalogue. Larger qualifying reductions approach 100.

Among qualifying candidates with positive scores, maximizing $S_c$ is exactly equivalent to minimizing $N_c$. Equal positive scores are resolved by smaller $E_c$. At zero, qualifying candidates precede rejected candidates and are ordered by $(N_c,E_c)$; this retains the original fallback to raw observations when no smaller representation qualifies. Exact objective ties remain equivalent. The ordering therefore reproduces equation~\ref{eq:selection}, including the zero-reduction boundary. No adjustable weight trades preservation against compactness. The threshold creates an intentional discontinuity: a tiny tolerance crossing can remove all credit, so discrepancy, tolerance and sensitivity results must accompany the score.

Every main panel reports the seven applicable families with unique recommendation positions 1--7. They are ordered by descending score and the numerical tie rules; exact numerical ties are finally resolved by their fixed position in the declared 15-family catalogue. The eight excluded families do not enter these scored tables. Within a family, every eligible setting is evaluated before its best setting is reported. Histograms and heatmaps each test 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 common bins; violins test 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256 density-grid points; dots test means and raw observations. Mean bars, five-number boxes and five-number intervals each have one defined setting. All 21 encoding settings and all 14 underlying numerical configurations are disclosed in the reproducibility tables. Supplementary Figures S5 and S6 show the numerical preservation checks.

Numerically equivalent families retain equal scores and discrepancies. Only the first recommended display is highlighted; its unique recommendation position must not be interpreted as a unique scientific optimum. A histogram and a binned heatmap can encode the same probabilities; mean bars and mean dots can encode the same means; box and five-number interval plots can encode the same quantiles. The algorithm records these scientific ties explicitly while also returning a unique recommendation order. The catalogue-order tie-break is a presentation choice, not a perceptually validated rule. Equal scores with different discrepancies remain ordered by discrepancy, as specified above.

For WDBC radius standard error, the eight-bin representation retains 18 of 569 values and scores 96.8; histogram and heatmap are tied encodings. For HAR gyroscope-jerk variability, the five-number representation retains 10 of 60 values and scores 83.3; box and five-number interval plots tie. For WDBC worst perimeter, an eight-point sampled-density representation retains 18 of 569 values and scores 96.8, selecting the violin family.\cite{wdbc,har} This last choice is provisional: conditional bootstrap stratum agreement is 0.714. The violin shows the actual eight-point density interpolation used by the decoder, not a more finely estimated curve. Mean representations fail the tolerance in all three examples and receive zero credit. The examples were selected after inspecting results and do not estimate reader benefit. Their variety illustrates genuine outcomes; chart families were not rewarded to force different winners.

Across the 127 primary comparisons, the selected representations are means in 61, five-number summaries in 29, binned distributions in 33 and sampled densities in four. Equivalent chart-family ties must not be counted as independent selections. The expanded encoding catalogue leaves all 149 numerical selections unchanged because it adds explicit equivalent displays and task screening, rather than new decoders or a new statistical target. The 22 Parkinsons comparisons remain excluded from the primary analysis.


The score is conditional on the target, tolerance, sample size and scalar-count contract. The same 18-value representation receives a higher reduction score against a larger raw sample. A high score can also result from a permissive adaptive tolerance. Consequently, cross-dataset score differences cannot establish better readability, stronger biological separation or better data quality. A zero score may mean that detailed observations are necessary, that a particular summary fails the target, or that no compression is offered; the qualification state distinguishes these cases.

\subsection*{Stress tests distinguish empirical reconstruction from population fidelity}
A new development stress test generated 1,080 independent training--test pairs: nine scenarios, three sample sizes and 40 replicates per setting. Scenarios included null, location, spread, bimodality, skewness, heavy tails, contamination, discreteness and a 1:4 group-size imbalance. The training sample selected a configuration using the compactness objective in equation~\ref{eq:selection}; the test sample refitted its retained values without changing its family or resolution. Comparators used fixed means, five-number summaries, raw observations, eight bins or an eight-point density grid. The latter two have identical retained size. These are transparent numerical baselines, not surrogate implementations of learned recommendation systems.

Three empirical checks were distinguished. The first re-estimated tolerance on the test sample. The second froze the training tolerance, preventing the test set from redefining the error budget. The third used a fixed $0.2s_{\mathrm{population}}$ budget in both samples; a separate selector was trained under that budget. The population scale is known in the simulation and is not an available oracle in ordinary use. The same fixed budget also assessed decoded contrasts against the generating population quantiles on 4,001 central probability points.

Adaptive selection passed \ReestimatedPass\% of test cases under re-estimated tolerance and \FrozenPass\% under frozen tolerance, with a median retained size of \MedianN. Raw observations passed both empirical checks exactly, with median size 160. The fixed eight-bin baseline passed 96.3\% of frozen-tolerance cases, exceeding the adaptive selector's 89.0\%, while retaining 18 rather than a median of two values. Thus the adaptive rule achieved greater compactness but did not maximize transfer fidelity. Under the fixed empirical budget, the separately trained fixed-budget selector passed 75.7\% of test cases. Adaptive selection and raw observations met the population budget in 20.2\% and 8.7\% of cases, respectively; aggregation across these scenarios does not establish general superiority. Table~\ref{tab:sim} and Supplementary Figure~S3 show the corresponding fixed-budget and population results. The separation of these endpoints is essential: a representation can faithfully encode a noisy empirical contrast while remaining inaccurate about the population. The pooled percentages average an equally weighted simulation design; they are not estimates of performance across scientific practice. Scenario-specific results and all failures are retained.

\subsection*{Encoding costs and source anomalies materially affect selection}
The representation contract is part of the method. Restoring two optional mean markers to the five-number cost changed 15 primary configurations. Counting histogram masses by their free parameters rather than their literal vector entries changed 18. Giving all representations equal cost changed all 127 configurations to exact empirical observations. This last comparison removes the compactness objective deliberately; it is a boundary case rather than a competing estimate of reader effort (Supplementary Fig.~S4a). The frontier, decoders and counts must therefore accompany any single recommendation.

Source quality can matter even when the target excludes extreme quantiles. Two CKD potassium values, 39 and 47 in source units, occur in source rows 62 and 129. Their original units and correctness could not be independently resolved. In a labelled sensitivity analysis, omitting those two observations changed the selected histogram from 32 to four bins, while the estimated tolerance remained 0.6 and distribution detail was still required (Supplementary Fig.~S4b,c). The observations remain unchanged in the primary data. Extreme values can affect range-based binning and summary decoders even when the target interval omits the outermost ranks.

The scope of the target also creates a strict limitation. Identical marginal distributions can have a zero quantile contrast while paired individuals change substantially. Reversing the order of the sample $(0,1,2,3)$ gives individual differences $(3,1,-1,-3)$ but unchanged marginals. A mean representation passes the declared marginal target exactly while concealing the individual changes (Supplementary Fig.~S4d). This counterexample is a reason to check the scientific question before using the selector, not a property that additional bootstrap draws can repair.




Figure~\ref{fig:limited} reports three limitations using the same selection score and 15-family catalogue. For BIDMC mean heart rate, means already preserve the target: retaining two of 92 values scores 97.8, so the method recommends no change in numerical detail; mean bars and mean dots remain equivalent. For CKD white blood cell count, four bins retain ten of 294 values and score 96.6, yet the long upper tail remains. For CKD potassium, 32 bins retain 66 of 312 values and score 78.8; the unresolved source extremes require more retained detail under this contract.\cite{bidmc,kidney} Neither a high nor a lower score diagnoses source quality. The omission sensitivity supports the narrower observation that these two potassium values affect the selected bin resolution. None of these examples establishes a data-driven ceiling on human visual performance, and this audit does not supply a near-zero optimum demonstrating such a ceiling.

\section*{Discussion}
This work makes numerical simplification accountable to a declared comparison. The preservation constraint prevents a small but inadequate summary from winning; the positive selection score makes the resulting reduction in retained detail easy to inspect. Exact retention alone receives no simplification credit. The useful output is not a universal winner among familiar chart names, but a record of the contrast to preserve, the tolerance accepted, the qualifying representations and the choices the numerical criterion cannot resolve. A researcher can see why a mean summary passes, when additional detail becomes necessary, and how that conclusion changes with resolution. The score remains a report of this constrained optimization, not a new perceptual metric. The deterministic display order cannot distinguish the visual effectiveness of numerically equivalent encodings; doing so requires an independently specified and validated perceptual criterion. Artificial score offsets would conceal this unresolved question.

The revised analysis identifies three distinct sources of disagreement. Monte Carlo uncertainty changes the estimated bootstrap allowance; scientific resolution changes what approximation error is acceptable; and the representation contract changes what counts as compact. The first was modest in this audit, whereas the latter two materially affected the selections. Reporting only a winning chart or a normalized score would hide those distinctions.

The framework complements existing recommendation approaches. Draco can express visual design constraints, and VizML can learn design patterns; neither comparison is reduced here to an unsupported claim of superiority on our reconstruction objective.\cite{draco,vizml} A numerical fidelity filter could precede an encoding recommender, but that integration has not been evaluated. Quantile-based group comparisons already provide a rich diagnostic vocabulary.\cite{rousselet2017} The present contribution is a reproducible way to connect that vocabulary to representation detail, with sensitivity paths rather than an assertion that one encoding is best.

Several limits remain consequential. The primary datasets were used during method development, their features are correlated, and the new stress tests were specified after reviewing the earlier results. They are expanded development evidence, not a preregistered external benchmark. Retinopathy records lack verified participant linkage; wrist summaries represent only eight participants. Ordinary bootstrap behaviour for discrete, dependent or small samples is not guaranteed by the observed convergence of random resampling. Equal-width bins, Scott-bandwidth densities and scalar payload counts are declared design choices. Floating-point membership at exact bin boundaries can also break numerical affine invariance, despite invariance in exact arithmetic.

Most importantly, reconstructing stored numbers is different from reading a chart. Axis choices, colour, clutter, labels and reader expertise affect interpretation.\cite{cleveland1984,crameri2020} A smaller representation need not be easier to understand, and a numerical pass does not justify omitting uncertainty required by the scientific question. Confidence-interval and standard-error displays are consequently outside the current objective. The eligible five-number interval display represents observed variability only; it is not a substitute for inferential uncertainty. The declared task screen also does not validate recommendation for temporal, relational, compositional, hierarchical or geographic objectives. The supplied prospective reader-study protocol defines randomized displays, estimation error, response time and confidence outcomes; it reports no recruited participants or measured benefit.

The next decisive test is to freeze a version, evaluate untouched data and measure whether its selected displays improve prespecified reading tasks. Until then, the practical claim is precise: \textbf{the procedure tells the researcher how much numerical detail is sufficient for a stated two-sample comparison at a stated resolution.} It makes the reasons for simplification available for inspection rather than asking the reader to trust a chart label.

\section*{Methods}
\subsection*{Data preparation and analysis units}
Inputs contain at least four finite values per group. Missing values are removed separately for independent samples and jointly for paired samples; pairing is declared and requires equal original lengths. WDBC contributes 30 variables, Cleveland five, heart failure six, CKD 11, ILPD nine, retinopathy 16, HAR 40, BIDMC five and wrist exercise five. HAR uses participant/activity medians from 30 participants; BIDMC aggregates monitor readings within 46 source patients; wrist exercise uses early--late ECG-reference summaries within eight participants.\cite{pimentel2017,jarchi2017} These are descriptive comparisons, not diagnostic-performance studies. Source copies, transformations, exclusions and checksums are bundled. The 22 Parkinsons results remain in an excluded exploratory table.

\subsection*{Decoders and exact empirical errors}
Empirical quantiles use linear interpolation. Means reconstruct a constant contrast. Five-number summaries interpolate through ranks $0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1$. Histograms use 4, 8, 16, 32 or 64 common equal-width bins spanning both samples; inverse cumulative interpolation reconstructs piecewise-uniform distributions. Densities use Scott-bandwidth Gaussian kernels evaluated at 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 or 256 common grid points, with a range extending four larger-group bandwidths beyond the sample range. Trapezoidal integration and normalization define the retained cumulative representation. Low-resolution densities qualify only if their resulting decoder meets the same empirical criterion. Raw observations reconstruct empirical quantiles exactly.

All empirical inverse decoders are piecewise linear, with possible jumps across empty histogram bins. Supremum errors are evaluated at the union of empirical and decoder knots, interval endpoints and adjacent floating-point values. This gives the supremum up to numerical precision; no sparse percentile grid is substituted for empirical qualification. Population simulation errors use a separate dense grid and are explicitly approximate. The pooled scale is the degrees-of-freedom-weighted within-group standard deviation, with the joint-range and unit fallback documented for constant inputs.

\subsection*{Resampling, sensitivity and simulation}
Resampling uses independent group indices or joint indices for paired data. The audit uses 999 draws, with nested 99- and 499-draw subsets and an independent 999-draw stream. Percentile, probability-range and fixed-budget sensitivities rerun their corresponding calculations. Conditional stratum agreement uses the original tolerance. The 0.80 flag is exploratory and is not a confidence probability. Cost sensitivities hold candidate errors and tolerances fixed while altering only their declared cost.

The simulation uses 20, 80 or 320 observations per group, except the 1:4 imbalance case. Forty independent replicates are generated per scenario and size. Training and test arrays are drawn independently from the same generating distributions. Each uses 499 bootstrap draws, selected as a computational compromise supported by the real-audit convergence check; this does not establish convergence in every simulated condition. All distributions, seeds, decoding functions and per-case results are given in Supplementary Methods and executable code. Tests check exact raw reconstruction, group-swap symmetry, affine invariance away from bin-boundary exceptions, monotonicity of the size--resolution path and empirical supremum calculations against dense-grid evaluations.
